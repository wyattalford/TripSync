# Core User Journey

John wants to watch Star Wars with Walker, Eli, and Noah on Friday. The last time they discussed it in their group chat, everyone sounded interested, but nobody settled on a time. John followed up once, then stopped because he did not want a casual invitation to feel like an obligation. They spent the evening separately and later discovered that everyone had been free. This time, John creates an OpenTime account and a group for the four friends. After the others register, he invites them by username, and each accepts membership.

John adds a Friday opening from 7 to 10 PM for watching a movie and shares it with this group. His friends publish their own openings for the same purpose. The group overview shows a shared opening from 7 to 10 PM without displaying anyone's individual schedule. John knows that a movie invitation would be welcome during that interval, but nobody has committed yet. He proposes watching Star Wars in the dorm lounge from 7 to 9:30 PM. Walker and Eli accept through their in-app invitations. Noah has not responded, so John confirms the gathering for himself, Walker, and Eli while Noah's invitation stays pending.

On Friday afternoon, John discovers that the lounge is booked until 7:30 PM. He edits the gathering to run from 7:30 to 10 PM. Before saving, OpenTime explains that the change will release the old commitments and require fresh acceptance and confirmation. John proceeds, and the gathering returns to a proposal. His friends receive notices asking them to review the revised details. Walker accepts the new time, but Eli declines because it no longer works for him. John sees these responses separately from Noah's unanswered invitation and confirms the revised gathering for himself and Walker. Eli's earlier acceptance has not been carried forward to the new time.

Noah later opens his invitation, reviews the current details, and accepts before the gathering starts. He joins the confirmed gathering immediately, and John receives a notice without needing to create or confirm another proposal. The attendees' committed time stops contributing to overlaps in their other groups, while the gathering's details remain visible only to its organizer and invitees. That evening, John, Walker, and Noah meet in the lounge and watch the movie. John has a definite time and attendee list, and an opportunity that previously disappeared into the group chat becomes an evening together.
