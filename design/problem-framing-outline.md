## Group 4: Solution Sketch and Ethical Analysis

### Solution Sketch

Explain the proposed workflow:

1. People share selected times when they welcome invitations and choose who can see them.
2. Friends discover overlapping openings.
3. Someone proposes an activity, time, and place.
4. Invitees accept or decline.
5. The group can distinguish a tentative idea from a confirmed plan.
6. Availability can be withdrawn or expire when it is no longer accurate.

Connect each behavior to the bad situations. Address whether maintaining availability would require less effort than current methods. Keep the sketch focused on behavior rather than detailed implementation.

### Ethical Analysis

Write **at least five insights, each 30–75 words**. Each insight must:

- Identify the dimension, an actual Envisioning Cards prompt, and the relevant feature.
- Explain a foreseeable consequence.
- Give a concrete design response.

Consider all four required dimensions: **Stakeholders, Time, Pervasiveness, and Values**. Multi-lifespan cards are not required.

Possible starting topics:

1. **Stakeholders — Friends who do not use the app:** Could they miss invitations as planning moves into OpenTime? Consider ways to include them.
2. **Time — Outdated availability:** Could misleading openings erode trust over time? Consider expiring availability and easy withdrawal.
3. **Pervasiveness — Expectations about personal time:** Could widespread use create pressure to make all unscheduled time available? Consider optional sharing and freedom to decline.
4. **Values — Privacy and connection:** Could sharing availability expose personal routines? Consider controls over who can see each opening.
5. **Values — Inclusion:** Could shorter openings help people with limited or irregular free time meet friends? Consider support for short, flexible gatherings.

These topics are starting points, not completed insights. Select the relevant toolkit prompts and consider benefits as well as harms. Use the team's suggested ethical concerns to inform the final analysis.
